# NEVER BROKEN
## The Hybrid Opening, drafted from Joe's nine beats
*Draft 1, 2026-08-23. Built on Joe's structural response of 2026-08-22 (see JOE-RESPONSES-2026-08-22.md section 5).*
*Approx. 90 seconds to the title card. Approx. 2 minutes including the bridge.*

---

## THE RULE THIS SCENE OBEYS

Open on the worst night of his life. Cut at the gun click. **Never show the release.** The audience leaves this scene not knowing whether four boys lived, and carries that question for an hour. When the film returns to the barn in Act One it plays the whole thing, alley through walk home, and every detail lands harder because we now know the father, the courtroom, and the town.

Joe's words: "I would not show the men releasing me in the opening. Once the audience knows I survive the barn, much of the narrative suspense disappears."

## COLD OPEN

> **FADE IN FROM BLACK.**

**BLACK SCREEN.**

No picture. Only sound, and it arrives before we have any idea where we are.

Breathing. Fast, shallow, four sets of it, close enough that we could touch them. Someone is crying and trying very hard not to. A floorboard shifts. Something heavy scrapes on dirt.

Then a voice, so quiet it is almost inside our own head. A boy's voice.

> **YOUNG JOE (V.O., whispered)**
> Lord. Lord, please.

A match strikes.

**INT. BARN, OUTSIDE MONROE, LOUISIANA. NIGHT. 1962.**

A lantern wick catches, and the dark opens maybe eight feet in every direction. That is the entire world for the next ninety seconds. Beyond it, nothing.

Four Black boys stand facing a barn wall. Thirteen, fourteen years old. Church shoes. One of them still has a bowling score card folded in his back pocket.

We do not see who is holding the lantern. We see what the lantern does.

**CLOSE. HANDS.** Four pairs, pressed flat against grey barn board. They are shaking hard enough that the wood ticks. One boy's hands are not flat. They are behind his back, wrists crossed, held there on their own, as though someone had already put cuffs on them once and the body remembered how to wait.

**CLOSE. FEET.** Scuffed shoes in the dirt, one heel lifting and setting down, lifting and setting down, the way a boy's foot does when the rest of him has been ordered not to move.

**CLOSE. A FACE.** JOE PROFIT, 13. Cheek against the board. Eyes open. Tears running sideways across the bridge of his nose, and he does not wipe them, because wiping them would mean moving.

Lantern shadows swing slow across all of it. Somewhere behind the boys, boots. Unhurried. A man enjoying the time this is taking.

> **MAN (O.S.)**
> Boy.

Joe's eyes close.

> **MAN (O.S.) (CONT'D)**
> I can make you do anything I want to.

The shadow of a raised arm crawls up the barn wall beside Joe's head and stops.

**SOUND. THE CYLINDER.** A revolver being cocked, four separate mechanical clicks, and the last one is the loudest thing in the film so far.

The three other boys stop breathing. All of them, at once.

**EXTREME CLOSE. JOE.** Thirteen years old. Eyes shut. Lips barely moving.

> **YOUNG JOE (whispered)**
> Lord, please.

**HARD CUT TO BLACK.**

Total silence. Not a fade, not a fall-off. The sound is amputated, and the black holds two full beats longer than an audience is comfortable with.

> **TITLE CARD: NEVER BROKEN**

## THE BRIDGE (present-day, Joe's own voice)

Hold on black under the title. Then, from the darkness, an older voice. Warm, unhurried, sixty years further down the road. This is Dr. Profit's actual recorded narration.

> **DR. JOE PROFIT (V.O.)**
> Long before I became a first-round draft pick, a businessman, or an author, I was a frightened boy standing inside a dark barn, wondering whether I would live to see another morning.

Beat.

> **DR. JOE PROFIT (V.O.) (CONT'D)**
> Before I can tell you how that night ended, I have to tell you about the morning.

The last line is written to hand the film to the curtain ritual and to promise the audience, out loud, that the barn is coming back. It is the contract. If we say it here we must pay it in Act One.

## MATCH CUT INTO ACT ONE

> **CAPTION: MONROE, LOUISIANA. SEVEN YEARS EARLIER.**

**INT. PROFIT FARMHOUSE, LAKE PROVIDENCE. DAWN. 1955.**

Black gives way to a seam of grey light, and the seam widens, because it is a curtain being pulled.

ETHEL PROFIT throws it wide over a room full of sleeping children.

> **ETHEL**
> Today is going to be a good day!

JOE, 5, squints into the light.

The rhyme is the point. Ninety seconds ago a lantern opened the dark on the worst night of this boy's life. Now a curtain opens the dark on his mother refusing to let the day be anything but good. Same move, opposite meaning, and the whole thesis of the film sits in the gap between them.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Why this opening is Seedance-friendly.** It is almost entirely close-ups of hands, feet, faces, and a swinging light source in an eight-foot pool of lantern glow. There is no wide establishing shot to keep consistent, no crowd, and one line of on-camera dialogue delivered by a man we never see. The hardest technical problem in the film, holding one face consistent across three ages, barely applies here, because Joe's face is in shadow and partial profile the entire time.

**The antagonist is never shown.** By design, and it is also free insurance: no captor face has to stay consistent when the scene returns in Act One, and the barn captors stay unnamed exactly as the treatment's license note requires.

**The one thing that must be shot twice.** The Act One return plays this same material from a wider, colder angle and continues past the gun. Generate both passes from the same reference set in the same session so the barn, the lantern, and the wardrobe match. If they drift, the payoff reads as a different night.

**Time captions.** Joe asked for them ("six hours later," "a week earlier"). Use them sparingly and only where a jump is genuinely ambiguous. The film has one significant non-chronological move, the barn, and captions on everything else will make a straightforward story feel restless.

## OPEN ITEMS THAT AFFECT THIS SCENE

1. **The bowling score card in the back pocket** is an invention. It is a one-second way to plant the alley without showing it. Confirm with Joe that a score card is period-plausible for that alley, or cut it.
2. **Crossed wrists** is likewise an invention, built off Joe's own note about hands appearing like handcuffs. He described it as a payoff for the courtroom. Planting a hint of it here makes the later callback land, but check that he is comfortable seeing it this early.
3. **"Boy, I can make you do anything I want to"** and **"Lord, please"** are quoted from Joe as though remembered verbatim. Confirm before locking, because verbatim lines get protected and reconstructed ones can be improved.
4. **The dawn location** depends on the unresolved Lake Providence question in JOE-RESPONSES-2026-08-22.md section 2. If the family had already moved to Monroe, this scene is a Monroe house and not a cotton farm, and the film loses its cover image.
