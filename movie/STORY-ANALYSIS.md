## 2. LOGLINE OPTIONS

1. **The Fields.** A sharecropper's son from the Louisiana cotton fields survives Jim Crow, breaks the color barrier in Southern college football, loses his NFL career to one tackle, and rebuilds himself into one of America's most successful Black entrepreneurs, proving a man can be bent by every field he walks and broken by none.
2. **The Vow.** Turned away from a burger joint's "White Entrance" at 15, a boy vows he will one day own the restaurant. He ends up owning the franchise, the phone company, and the room where a President decides to sign the MLK holiday into law.
3. **The Comeback.** (Sports-forward) The 7th pick of the 1971 draft shreds his knee in his fourth NFL game. Doctors say he will never walk without a limp. He walks into boardrooms instead.

Option 1 is the movie the book wrote. Option 2 is the best trailer hook for the fundraise.

## 2.5 THE CHOSEN ARC (decision, 7/13: sports arc, not full life)

**Decision:** The film covers 1949-1971, cotton field to draft day. Everything after football becomes epilogue title cards. Rationale: the full-life version peaks at the draft and goes anticlimactic through the business years; struggle-to-victory is the engine, and this cut keeps the engine running to the last scene. We do not cover the whole book. Creative license in dialogue is approved; composite characters OK; the flagged facts in section 6 still must be accurate because of the fundraise.

**Dramatic question:** Can a boy born into a system engineered to break him make it out unbroken?

```
ACT ONE, THE SYSTEM (~25 min)                    1949-1966, ages 5-16
  Cold open: the barn, gun cocked. Black. TITLE.
  Curtains: "Today is going to be a good day!" Cotton rows. 9 kids.
  Grandma's peonage stories. Cousin Henry died in a labor camp.
  Neighbor Thomas disputes the rigged cotton scale -> chain gang.
  Magic shoes crumble -> a season in work boots -> "Joe the Jet."
  Pigeon business. Coach Robinson: "the mind of a businessman."
  Age 14: handcuffed for "unlawful thinking." Courtroom. Mother sings.
  Burger Chef "White Entrance." The silent vow.
  THE BARN, full scene. Walk home on the tracks.
  ACT TURN: "What began as survival became purpose."

ACT TWO, THE DOOR (~50 min)                      1966-1970, ages 17-21
  Richwood: Coach Freeze, the 26-0 halftime speech, the comeback.
  Freeze refuses the technicality trophy. (integrity installed)
  Alcorn: filthy dorm, broken promises. Greyhound home.
  Kitchen confrontation. Father: "Go on and give it a shot."
  NLU tryout. Dixie White: "This is a white school."
  First practice: silence, stares, the slur, the fight.
  White "looks the other way." Joe wins the fight, helps him up.
  Cafeteria: eats alone -> "swarm him like bees" -> first laugh.
  MIDPOINT: bottles thrown at Stephen F. Austin -> 95-yard return
    -> the SAME crowd chants "Go Joe Go." Proof it can be done.
  Backlash: benched at every goal line. Dr. Garrett: "Never more
    than a D, boy." Home friends: "sellout." Alone on both sides.
  ALL IS LOST: Joe packs the footlocker to quit. Groseclose folds
    his shirts into the case: "You'll be the guy who cut and ran."
  Recommit: track montage, 9.5 dash, records fall, first start,
    father in the stands at last.

ACT THREE, THE GAME OF HIS LIFE (~25 min)        1970-1971, ages 21-22
  Father hospitalized: "Go have the game of your life."
  Hammond. Halftime PA: "Joe Profit's father has died."
  Collapse on the turf. "Go home, Joe." He stays. Plays the
    greatest game of his career in tears. (EMOTIONAL CLIMAX)
  Teammates who once fought him carry him through the grief.
  Senior montage: All-America. Marries Sunday, class Monday.
  CLIMAX: draft day, family around the radio. Misses the Jets
    call buying a Coke he doesn't want. Phone rings. Falcons. 7th.
  RESOLUTION: Joe Profit Day. No. 40 retired, first in school
    history. Coach White at the podium. The town that jeered
    him fills the hall. (the racism arc, not the football arc,
    is what resolves here; this is the real victory scene)

EPILOGUE CARDS (~2 min)                          1971-today
  Every card is another field he won. See section 6.
```

**Why the ending is not a cheat:** the draft is the external win, but Joe Profit Day is the emotional win: the same community that handcuffed a 14-year-old for "unlawful thinking" stands and applauds him. Conflict -> struggle -> vindication, ending inside the victory.

**Why the epilogue cards land instead of deflating:** the first card is a gut punch, not a victory lap: "Four games into his rookie season, Joe shattered his knee. Doctors said he'd never walk without a limp." Beat. Then card after card of what he built anyway (Inc. 500 x3, first minority firm in Kuwait's reconstruction, the MLK holiday phone call, ordained minister, kids published before they can drive). The cards PROVE the title: football didn't make him, so losing it couldn't break him. That is more inspiring as 60 seconds of text than as 40 minutes of boardroom scenes.

**Optional frame device:** open and close at the 1999 Louisiana Sports Hall of Fame induction, elder Joe at the podium telling the story. Gives the film a present-tense "legend" bookend and a natural voiceover source (his audiobook narration), and gets HoF admittance on screen instead of in a card. Costs one extra character age. Decide at treatment stage.

**Production bonus for Seedance:** this cut needs 3 character ages instead of 6 (child, teen, college) plus optional elder, and two period looks (1950s farm/town, late-60s campus/stadiums) instead of seven. Cheaper, faster, more consistent.

## 3. WHERE THE STORY BEGINS

**Recommendation: cold open in the barn, 1965. Then begin the story in the cotton field.**

- **Cold open:** night, a remote barn outside Monroe. Four Black teenagers lined against the wall by lantern light. A revolver cocks next to 16-year-old Joe's head. A voice: "You're in our country." Hold. Cut to black. TITLE: NEVER BROKEN.
- **Scene 1 proper:** dawn, Lake Providence, 1950s. Ethel Profit flings open the curtains over her nine children: **"Today is going to be a good day!"** Barefoot Joe walks into first light over endless cotton rows (this is literally the book's cover image, the split photo of boy and man).

Why this works:
- The barn is the single most tense scene in the book and it happens at age 16. Opening there buys you 30 minutes of patience for the childhood material, because the audience knows where it's heading.
- The mother's curtain ritual against that opening is the whole thesis in 60 seconds: a system built to break them, and the family's daily refusal.
- It gives the fundraise trailer its first 20 seconds for free.

**Alternative (frame story):** open present-day, elder Dr. Profit walking a cotton field at dawn in a tailored suit, voiceover from the book's introduction ("Every field I've walked has strengthened me for the next"). Return to him between acts. Lower tension, but easier in Seedance (voiceover carries scenes, fewer lip-sync closeups) and lets Joe narrate in his own recorded voice, which we already have 22 audiobook tracks of. **These two are combinable: cold open barn, then elder Joe VO takes over.**

Do NOT begin at birth/chronological crawl, and do not begin at the NFL draft (his football fame is the least distinctive part of the story; the fields are the story).

## 4. FULL-BOOK MAP (reference only; superseded by section 2.5 for the film)

Kept as the master chronology for the treatment, epilogue cards, and any future sequel/doc-series. The book's own metaphor: five fields. The FILM uses fields 1-2; fields 3-5 become the epilogue.

| Act | Field | Years | Joe's age | Core events | Act turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | **The Cotton Field** | 1949-1967 | 0-18 | Peonage family history, mother's ritual, magic-shoes/work-boots season, pigeon business, arrest for "unlawful thinking" at 14, Burger Chef "White Entrance" vow, the barn | Walks off Alcorn's broken promises, walks ONTO the all-white NLU practice field |
| II | **The Football Field** | 1967-1971 | 18-22 | Daily fights, slur on the tackle, coach "looks the other way," cafeteria isolation ends, Dr. Garrett's "never more than a D," near-quit stopped by Coach Groseclose, 95-yard kickoff return flips a hostile crowd | Father dies, announced over the stadium PA at halftime. Football ends. The plan made in the hospital bed begins |

**Emotional midpoint of the film:** the father's death at halftime (end Act II). Everything before is proving himself to the world; everything after is deciding who he is without the man who taught him.

**Thematic rhyme to protect in every draft:** Burger Chef back entrance (Act I) -> owns Atlanta's top Burger Kings (Act IV). Eats alone in the cafeteria (Act II) -> lifelong rule "never eat alone" -> feeding kids' minds (Act V). Cotton scale rigged against his neighbor (Act I) -> jury rules for CII against the Army (Act IV).

## 5. WHERE THE STORY ENDS

**Decision (7/13): the film ends at Joe Profit Day, 1971, the No. 40 jersey retirement.** Draft day is the climax; the retirement ceremony is the resolution, because it resolves the racism arc: the community that jeered him fills the hall. Then epilogue cards (section 6), opening with the knee injury as a gut-punch beat before the victory cards. Optional 1999 Hall of Fame frame bookends it in the present tense.

Closing VO over the final scene or first card, from the book's actual closing: **"Success isn't the goal. The pursuit is what defines your goal. Let's stay Never Broken."**

(Superseded earlier recommendation, kept for the record: ending at the 2015 ordination. Right for the book, wrong for the film; it plays anticlimactic after the draft. If a doc-series or sequel ever covers the business/ministry years, the ordination quiet-room scene is its ending.)

## 6. TRAILING FOOTNOTES (epilogue title cards)

Over archival-style stills / slow Seedance pushes. **Verified facts only on screen; investors and press will check these.**

CARD ORDER (decision 7/13): open with the injury as a gut punch, then build. Every card after #1 is another field he won; the sequence proves the title.

SAFE TO PUT ON SCREEN (independently verified):
1. (FIRST CARD, the turn) Four games into his rookie season, Joe shattered his knee. Doctors said he would never walk without a limp. He was 22.
2. So he built. His company, Communications International Inc., was named one of America's fastest-growing private companies by Inc. Magazine three years in a row: 800+ employees, 34 cities, 9 countries.
3. In 1991, CII became the first minority-owned firm awarded a multimillion-dollar contract in the reconstruction of Kuwait. He used it to give U.S. troops free calls home.
4. The boy once turned away from a restaurant's "White Entrance" came to own the top Burger King franchises in Atlanta.
5. Inducted into the Louisiana Sports Hall of Fame, 1999.
6. In 2018 he won his party's primary for the U.S. Congress in Georgia's 4th District.
7. Ordained into ministry, 2015. His Youth United for Prosperity foundation has turned schoolchildren in Kentucky, Alabama, Louisiana and beyond into published authors before they could drive.
8. Dr. Joe Profit is still in the field.
9. (Fundraise card) 100% of proceeds from "Never Broken" go to the YUP Foundation.

USE ONLY WITH JOE'S DOCUMENTATION (found only in self-published bios, or conflicting):
- "Largest contract ever awarded to a U.S. minority-owned company." Research found sources saying FIRST, not LARGEST. The book says largest. Get Joe's paper before putting the superlative on screen or in the investor deck.
- Ernst & Young / Venture Magazine "Entrepreneur of the Year," USA Today "Best and Brightest," advised "U.S. Presidents" (plural). All plausible, all only self-sourced so far.
- First African-American IHOP franchisee: sources split between "in Georgia" and "in the Southeast." Pick the narrower claim.
- Total count of kids published by Young Authors. No aggregate number exists publicly. Get it from Joe; it may be the single best stat in the whole pitch.

RESEARCH GAP THAT NEEDS JOE: no press coverage of him from 2023-2026 was findable. The "what he's doing today" card must come from Joe directly (current YUP activity, the book launch itself, speaking, the movie fundraise). Interview him for this; it is also the strongest possible epilogue: not a legacy card, a "still going" card.

## 7. FACT & TIMELINE FLAGS (before treatment)

1. **Chapters 3 and 4 tell the same events twice** (arrest + barn). Use Chapter 4's detailed version; do not duplicate on the timeline.
2. **Operation Phone Home dating**: the book places it during the 1991 Gulf War via YUP, but YUP was founded 1995/96 (IRS record). Ask Joe which entity ran it in '91, or just show the act without naming the foundation.
3. **The ordination (2015) predates the congressional run (2018)**, but the book narrates ordination last. Fine for the film (thematic order beats chronology in Act V), but the epilogue cards must carry the 2018 run so nothing on screen is chronologically false.
4. Born Aug 13, 1949, Lake Providence, LA. Father: Simon Profit Sr. (WWII vet, d. ~1969-70 during Joe's college years). Mother: Ethel. Fifth of nine children.
5. The book credits "Dayrl Young" for photography. Yes, that's you, spelled wrong on the Amazon listing. Worth fixing with the distributor.

## 8. SEEDANCE PRODUCTION NOTES (for the shot bible later)

- **One protagonist, three ages** with the chosen 1949-1971 arc (child ~8, teen 16, college 18-22), plus optional elder Joe if the 1999 HoF frame is used. Character consistency across ages is THE technical risk. Build a locked character reference set (face identity kit per age) before generating a single scene.
- **Period looks needed (chosen arc):** 1950s Louisiana cotton farm and small town, 1960s segregated Monroe (bowling alley, courtroom, barn, Burger Chef), late-60s college campus and stadiums. Draft day is a living room and a radio. No NFL game footage needed at all (the film ends before his first pro snap; uniforms/trademark problem disappears).
- **Seedance-friendly scene types this story is full of:** dawn landscapes, stadium wides, crowd reactions, montages, archival-style stills. **Seedance-hostile:** long dialogue two-handers (courtroom, locker room speeches). Push dialogue into VO from the audiobook wherever possible; Joe already narrated the whole book in his own voice, and that voice is the film's greatest free asset.
- The trailer for the fundraise needs: barn cold open, curtain ritual, boots on the practice field, the 95-yard return with the crowd flipping, halftime PA announcement and the collapse, draft-day radio. Six shots. That's the pitch.
