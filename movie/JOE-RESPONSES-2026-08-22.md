## 2. THE FATHER AND THE GI BILL (partially answered, still open)

**What Joe said:** the GI Bill period was after the war ended, around 1946, not 1967. At the time his father said "Son, I could lose my job if you do this," the family had been in Monroe for years and his father was working for a **construction company**, not sharecropping.

**STILL OPEN, and it matters:** Joe did not answer the denied-versus-received question. The treatment currently states the GI Bill "did not apply to men who looked like him." That is a factual claim about his father that will be read as history, so it needs Joe's direct yes.

**NEW CONTRADICTION, must resolve before the screenplay.** Joe wrote that the family "had moved from the farm in Lake Providence to Monroe over 20 years earlier." Measured back from 1967 that is 1947, which is two years before Joe was born in Lake Providence, and it would mean he had no cotton-field childhood at all. Act One opens on that farm with five-year-old Joe. Either the move was later than Joe remembers, or Act One's entire setting is wrong. Ask him for the year the family left Lake Providence.

## 5. STRUCTURE: JOE SIDES WITH THE CURRENT DRAFT

This is the important one, and it is good news. Joe read Dave's note and landed on a **hybrid opening** that is essentially what the treatment already does, with more specificity.

**What Joe agrees with:**
- Open on the barn. It is the most defining moment in his life, not merely a dramatic beat
- Do NOT reveal the full incident or its outcome up front
- Do NOT show the men releasing the boys. Once the audience knows he survives, the suspense is gone
- Do NOT open with the whole bowling-alley sequence either. It needs the childhood and the historical context to carry its moral weight
- Return to the complete sequence later, alley through abduction, prayer, barn, release, walk home

**Joe's nine-beat opening,** drafted out in OPENING-SCENE.md:
1. Darkness. Frightened breathing, quiet crying, a whispered prayer
2. A lantern ignites, revealing four young Black boys facing the barn wall
3. Camera stays close. Trembling hands, quivering feet, tears, lantern shadows
4. A man: "Boy, I can make you do anything I want to."
5. The unmistakable click of the gun
6. Young Joe closes his eyes: "Lord, please."
7. Cut immediately to black
8. TITLE: NEVER BROKEN
9. Into the childhood, chronological from there

**Where Joe pushes back on the current draft:**
- He wants the dynamite up front and worries about a short-attention-span audience
- He wants **time captions** ("six hours later," "a week earlier") to hold that audience
- He wants some present-day material threaded through rather than held entirely to the end. This is the argument FOR the optional elder-Joe frame device already parked in STORY-ANALYSIS section 2.5. Joe has effectively voted yes on it
- He offered a present-day bridge line to follow the title card:

> "Long before I became a first-round draft pick, a businessman, or an author, I was a frightened boy standing inside a dark barn, wondering whether I would live to see another morning."

**The payoffs Joe wants protected in the later full version:**
- Calling the armed man "sir" reads as a final tribute to his father
- His hands held like handcuffs connect the abduction back to the courtroom
- Those same hands folding into prayer mark helplessness turning into faith
- "Come on, boys. Let's get out of here" is a repeated line, spoken first in defeat at the bowling alley and again after surviving the barn
- His belief that the Spirit of the Lord intervened is faith lived under mortal danger, not discussed afterward
